What Can You Make With Stock?

Sauces

As I mentioned on the first page, sauces are one of the things that can be made from stock. There are more sauces out there than I could ever hope to learn how to make, let alone list here. Sauces are split into two main categories: "Mother/Greater" sauces, and "Daugther/Lesser" Sauces. Mother sauces are base sauces that all Daughter sauces are made from. In modern French Cuisine, there are 5 "Mother Sauces" that are taught to chefs.

  • Béchame
  • Espagnole
  • Velouté
  • Tomato
  • Hollandaise

The first 4 sauces on this list use some type of stock in their recipe. Hollandaise being the outlier here. Historically, Hollandaise has not always been considered a Mother Sauce. It is said that Chef Auguste Escoffier's 1907 English Edition of his book "Le Guide Culinaire" is where it was first described as a Mother Sauce.

Three of these Mother Sauces, and their subsequent Daughter Sauces use stock in their base. Velouté is a light sauce that uses White Stock and thickened with a light roux. Velouté is the french word for 'velvety'. Some of the Daughter sauces made with a velouté are Sauce vin Blanc, Suprême sauce, and Allemande. The majority of dishes served with a velouté or its derivatives contain light meats such as fish or poultry, typically matching the bones used to make the stock. Tomato Sauce also uses a white stock in its base, but using veal or beef bones to make it. As its name suggests, it uses tomatoes to create a rich and thick sauce. This is the base of your common pasta and pizza sauces. The last Mother sauce I'll talk about is Espagnole. This sauce calls for a Brown Stock made from veal, beef and/or ham bones. Espagnole has a strong meat-y and tomato-y flavor.It was named 'Espagnole' due to its signature red color that is associated with the country of Spain. Some of its derivatives include sauce Bourguignonne and sauce Robert.

Soups and Stews

In my opinion, the best way to use home-made stock is to just make it into a soup or stew. I feel like this is the best way to experience the full depth of flavor the stock provides. The main difference between soup and stew is the thickness of it. Soups tend to have more liquid with smaller chunks of ingredients while stews are thicker with larger chunks of ingredients. Stews also differ from soup as they are cooked more slowly to allow all of the flavors to meld together. There are also chowders and bisques. Chowders differ from the rest as they are specifically cream-based. They tend to have chunkier ingredients like stews. Biques are smooth and creamy, but this effect is achieved by pureeing the ingredients used. Any of these options are a fantastic way to showcase the hard work you put into whatever stock you made.
